[font_size=26][color=#f0c96a][b]Pentakosmos[/b][/color][/font_size]
[i]Every player runs an academy. By collecting the five elements and studying the cards that embody them, you shape your own path through the cosmos — and the great philosophers come to teach at the academy that best reflects their ideas.[/i]

[font_size=22][color=#f0c96a]The five elements[/color][/font_size]
Each element is a Platonic solid: [b]air[/b] (octahedron), [b]water[/b] (icosahedron), [b]aether[/b] (dodecahedron), [b]fire[/b] (tetrahedron) and [b]earth[/b] (cube). The [b]sphere[/b] is the joker and counts as any element.

[font_size=22][color=#f0c96a]Setup[/color][/font_size]
• Element tokens per pile: 4 / 6 / 7 / 8 for 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 players.
• Spheres: 4 / 5 / 6 / 7 for 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 players.
• Element cards come in three groups: basic, intermediate and advanced. The market shows 4 cards per group (5 per group with 5 players).
• Philosophers on the table: number of players + 1.

[font_size=22][color=#f0c96a]Your turn: do one of these[/color][/font_size]
• [b]Take tokens:[/b] 1, 2 or 3 tokens of different elements; or 2 tokens of the same element if that pile has at least 3.
• [b]Reserve a card:[/b] take a card from the market (or the top card of a pile, face down) into your hand of reserves and gain 1 sphere, if any are left. You can hold at most 3 reserved cards.
• [b]Buy a card:[/b] from the market or from your reserves. Each card you own is a permanent discount of 1 in its element, so you pay the cost minus your cards of each element; spheres replace any missing token. The tokens you pay go back to the piles.

[font_size=22][color=#f0c96a]Token limit[/color][/font_size]
At the end of your turn you may hold at most 10 tokens (the menu allows 9 to 11). Return the extra ones.

[font_size=22][color=#f0c96a]Philosophers[/color][/font_size]
At the end of your turn, a philosopher joins your academy if your cards meet the requirement shown on the tile (at most one per turn; if several qualify, you choose).
• [b]−2 any:[/b] the total requirement is 2 cards lower, and those 2 can be missing from any elements.
• [b]Points 3·2·1:[/b] your first philosopher is worth 3 points, the second 2, and each one after that 1. The number at the top left of each tile shows what it would give you now; the number in the corner shows how many cards you still need.

[font_size=22][color=#f0c96a]Time per move[/color][/font_size]
Optionally, each move has a time limit, set separately for games against AIs (no limit by default) and for games between people (60 seconds by default). The ring above the Menu button shows the time left. When it runs out, the turn passes; if a token must be returned or a philosopher chosen, the game decides for you.

[font_size=22][color=#f0c96a]End of the game[/color][/font_size]
The last round starts when a player reaches [b]15 points[/b], or achieves the [b]specialization victory[/b]: 10 points from cards of a single element (philosophers do not count). The round is completed so that everyone has played the same number of turns.
• Whoever achieved the specialization victory beats whoever did not.
• Then, most points wins; on a tie, whoever bought fewer cards.

[font_size=22][color=#f0c96a]The Pentakosmos deck[/color][/font_size]
The 90 cards are generated by 18 formulas. Each formula defines a card's group, points and costs relative to its element, following the cycle air → water → aether → fire → earth → air, so every formula produces the same card for each of the five elements: no element is favoured. The "Creative process" page tells how it was designed.

[font_size=22][color=#f0c96a]Governors (optional)[/color][/font_size]
With governors on, the goal of players without a governor goes up (19 points by default). At the start, each player chooses a governor in 30 seconds (or the game draws one), and the chosen ones go to the table, next to the reserves. Choosing gives no exclusivity: at the end of a turn, whoever meets a governor's condition first may receive it — or refuse. Each governor has a condition, sometimes a cost, sometimes an advantage, and changes the goal of whoever receives it: Pericles, for example, asks for 2 advanced cards and lets you win with 15 points. Each player can have only one. A refused governor can be claimed later by tapping it on your turn. At the end, whoever reached their own goal beats whoever did not.
The base game brings Pericles, Leonidas and Alexander; the expansion brings 14 more.

[font_size=22][color=#f0c96a]Special cards[/color][/font_size]
Custom decks (expansion) can have cards with a sphere cost (paid only with spheres), negative points, a bonus of several elements, no bonus, or a joker (when you buy it, you choose the element of one of your cards). Some cards have an effect when bought: take 1 or 2 tokens, take 1 sphere or play again — the symbol is in the corner of the card.

[font_size=22][color=#f0c96a]Mix and match[/color][/font_size]
Every rule above can be changed separately in the start menu (and saved as your own preset): for example the points to win (1 to 100), or adding the writers of the Archaic mode to the academy.
